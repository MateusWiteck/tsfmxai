\subsection{Corrected terminal sampling and generation cost}
\label{sec:multivariate-generation-results}
An exploratory hybrid-channel benchmark adds univariate KernelSynth draws as
exogenous sources for AST channels. It fixes eight DataLoader workers on the
local CPU and delivers batches of 16 examples, six channels, 96 context steps,
and 96 future steps. After 16 warmup batches, 64 batches are measured at each
per-channel KernelSynth probability. The measured quantity is elapsed delivery
time divided by 64, with the consumer continuously requesting batches; it is
not GPU stall or full training time. The executed experiment record is in
\texttt{experiments/generator\_model\_evaluation/kernel\_synth\_hybrid/}.
\begin{table}[htbp]\centering

0\% & 0.0\% & 0.190 \\

75\% & 74.8\% & 0.189 \\\hline

These four observations do not show a monotonic cost increase. They are one
ordered run per probability, so machine load and prefetch can affect the small
differences. The compatibility implementation still samples an unused AST for
each KernelSynth channel, and caches kernel factorizations within each worker
while drawing fresh GP trajectories. This benchmark establishes only the
observed batch delivery cost of that implementation, not a forecasting gain.
It cannot be directly compared with the earlier TimePFN LMC cold-update timing:
this benchmark draws approximately 208-point trajectories with one window per
system and no model step, whereas the pinned TimePFN runner draws 1,024-point
trajectories, caches them, extracts multiple windows, and its cold timing
includes a GPU update. The hybrid path uses the official kernel bank in a
separate sampler, not the pinned TimePFN sampling implementation.

The corrected sampler assigns terminal types during tree construction. Its
default lag probability starts at 25\% for self-only channels and approaches
75\% as the effective source count grows. Table~\ref{tab:terminal-sampling}
reports paired before/after runs with seeds 314159, 271828 and 161803 under
the diagnostic mixture in Section~\ref{sec:multivariate-generation}. Each run
contains 500 accepted systems and 4,000 windows. Entries are mean $\pm$
sample standard deviation across the three seeds ($n=3$, denominator $n-1$).
They include variability in sampled mechanisms and runtime. The execution order
is fixed and process load can affect timing.

The treated-only eight-worker stage delivered batches of 32 windows in
53.0--57.9 ms on average after 20 warmup batches.
Each topology was measured over 100 batches. This stage uses zero originals,
50\% Shao--Wu, one sixth Jacobian and one third Heaps. The original-channel
terminal rule has no active channels in this stage. Throughput includes
prefetch and serialization. Hardware load and single-seed timing limit comparisons.


A new paired univariate evaluation used 1,000 candidates per policy and 256
generated steps, with at most four initial histories. Corrected sampling
accepted 963 candidates, including 247 lag-free equations and 293 distinct
canonical supports. Uniform sampling accepted 637 candidates, with 637 supports
and zero lag-free equations. These support counts describe differently sized
accepted samples. They are descriptive structural coverage measurements.

Table~\ref{tab:paired-terminal-sampling} restricts the comparison to accepted
original equations with lag memory, yielding 716 corrected-sampling pairs for
Shao--Wu and 637 uniform-sampling pairs. Each method receives the same history
and bounded innovations as its original. Trajectories are standardized separately.
The ACF error averages absolute differences over lags 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16.

Corrected sampling produces accepted original trajectories whose median paired
similarity to Shao--Wu is high even after excluding lag-free equations. Individual
pairs can differ substantially. Shared innovations may increase correlation.
Heaps constructs a different scalar AR and therefore measures a different
mechanism comparison. The bounded-noise control differs from default Gaussian
innovations for Shao--Wu and Heaps. This study covers finite-horizon univariate
trajectories. Distributional coverage of the multivariate treated-only mixture
requires a separate evaluation. The complete timing record and executed paired
notebook are stored in the experiment directory
