\subsection{Generation cost}
\label{sec:multivariate-generation-results}
An exploratory hybrid-channel benchmark adds univariate KernelSynth draws as
exogenous sources for AST channels. It fixes eight DataLoader workers on the
local CPU and delivers batches of 16 examples, six channels, 96 context steps,
and 96 future steps. After 16 warmup batches, 64 batches are measured at each
per-channel KernelSynth probability. The measured quantity is elapsed delivery
time divided by 64, with the consumer continuously requesting batches; it is
not GPU stall or full training time. The executed experiment record is in
\texttt{experiments/generator\_model\_evaluation/generator\_selection/kernel\_synth\_hybrid\_assessment/}.
\begin{table}[htbp]\centering
\caption{Exploratory hybrid generator throughput on one local CPU run.}
\label{tab:hybrid-kernelsynth-throughput}
\begin{tabular}{rrr}\hline
Configured KernelSynth probability & Realized channel fraction & Seconds/batch \\\hline
0\% & 0.0\% & 0.190 \\
25\% & 25.1\% & 0.198 \\
50\% & 49.7\% & 0.203 \\
75\% & 74.8\% & 0.189 \\\hline
\end{tabular}\end{table}
These four observations do not show a monotonic cost increase. They are one
ordered run per probability, so machine load and prefetch can affect the small
differences. The measured version also sampled an AST for every KernelSynth
channel, which the generator now skips, so these timings are an upper bound for
that cost. Kernel factorizations are cached within each worker while fresh GP
trajectories are drawn. This benchmark establishes only batch delivery cost,
not a forecasting gain.
It cannot be directly compared with the TimePFN LMC cold-update timing:
this benchmark draws approximately 208-point trajectories with one window per
system and no model step, whereas the pinned TimePFN runner draws 1,024-point
trajectories, caches them, extracts multiple windows, and its cold timing
includes a GPU update. The hybrid path uses the official kernel bank in a
separate sampler, not the pinned TimePFN sampling implementation.

For the prospective 96/96 forecasting shape, we reran CPU-only AST batch
delivery with eight workers, 16 six-channel examples per batch, 16 warmup
batches, and 64 measured batches per topology. Each of the 16 systems supplies
one newly generated 192-step trajectory and one window. The generator uses
one to three AST operators, lag cap 16, and treatments drawn independently of
the topology (50\% Shao--Wu, one sixth Jacobian, one third Heaps). The complete executed
configuration and timing record are in
\texttt{experiments/multivariate\_generation/07\_ast\_batch\_96\_96/}.
\begin{table}[htbp]\centering
\caption{AST-only online batch delivery at the prospective 96/96 shape.}
\label{tab:ast-batch-96-throughput}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}\hline
Topology & Seconds/batch & Batches/s \\\hline
Independent & 0.192 & 5.22 \\
Random & 0.170 & 5.89 \\
Predecessor chain & 0.172 & 5.83 \\\hline
\end{tabular}\end{table}
These timings describe a pilot with one to three operators, uniform startup,
and a 192-step trajectory. The training arm uses one to six operators, the
dependency-driven treatment rule, and randomly sampled startup histories, so
its throughput is measured separately before it is used to project training
duration. These timings include prefetch and interprocess serialization, but no
model step or GPU transfer. Each topology was run once in fixed order, so the
small differences do not establish a ranking.
