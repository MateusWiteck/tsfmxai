\subsection{Synthetic generators as forecasting priors}
\label{sec:foundation-prior-design}

Our primary question is which complete generation-and-training pipeline
delivers the better zero-shot forecaster under the same machine-time budget.
It rewards inexpensive generation and higher throughput. A complementary
exposure-controlled study asks which generator supplies more useful data when
the number of examples and optimizer updates are equal. The LMC arm samples
windows from a finite trajectory bank, whereas our generator draws new
systems for successive batches. This secondary study does not match unique
trajectory counts. It therefore cannot isolate the content of the synthetic
mechanisms from the diversity of trajectories. In particular, a tie here and
a win in the time-limited study would not establish that speed alone caused
the difference.
Neither comparison by itself
identifies which property of the synthetic mechanisms caused a difference. The
controlled-corpus design follows \citet{nagpal2026mixdontpick}; budget-aware
training follows \citet{kaddour2023notrainnogain}.

\subsubsection{Chronos-2 for the equation head}
\label{sec:chronos-explainability-modifications}
The auxiliary equation head described below must recover, for every series,
which lags and which other series its generating equation uses. The published
Chronos-2 represents individual time steps inside its patches, but it cannot
distinguish the series of a group. We therefore keep its time patching and add
one change, a channel-index embedding, only in the arms that train the
equation head.

\paragraph{Time attention: 16-step patches.}
Chronos-2 splits each series into non-overlapping patches of 16 steps, maps
each patch to one embedding with a residual network, and applies rotary
position embeddings (RoPE) in its time attention
\citep[p.~5]{ansari2025chronos2}. The residual network receives the 16 values
in fixed input positions, so the step at lag $t-3$ and the step at lag $t-7$
enter through different weights rather than being averaged. With a 96-step
context, all lags up to 16 used by the generator lie in the last context patch,
each at a fixed position, and the time attention relates patches. Recovering a
specific lag therefore requires the backbone to learn it from the patch
embedding instead of addressing the step directly; this is a difference of
inductive bias, not an architectural impossibility. One-step patches would make
every step addressable by RoPE, but they multiply the tokens per series from
13 to 193 and, in our measurement on the RTX~4070, the time per update by about
12.5. Patching also improves forecasting efficiency and accuracy
\citep{nie2023patchtst}. We keep 16-step patches, and we use the head's lag
recovery on synthetic data to check that the patch representation retains the
lags.

\paragraph{Group attention: channel index.}
Group attention aggregates information across the series of one example at
each token index. It omits positional embeddings because the series of a
group lack a natural ordering \citep[p.~6]{ansari2025chronos2}. Its outputs
are therefore equivariant to permutations of the channels: the model cannot
distinguish channel 0 from channel 3. An equation target that names series by
their channel index, such as $x_3(t-1)$, is then not learnable, because the
same series placed in two positions produces the same representation but
requires two different targets. We therefore add a learned embedding of the
absolute channel index, $0,\ldots,5$, to every input token of the
corresponding series, once, before the first encoder layer. This follows
the original Transformer, which adds position information to the input
embeddings at the bottom of the stack and reports nearly identical results
for learned and fixed position embeddings \citep[Section~3.5 and Table~3,
row~E]{vaswani2017attention}. Applying it to channel identity rather than
token position is our adaptation. Two properties of the pinned architecture
motivate the input placement. First, each sublayer adds its output to a
residual stream, so the embedding added at the input remains present at the
input of every group attention layer; group attention can then carry the
identity of a source series in its values to the target series. Second,
within one series every token receives the same embedding, so in time
attention its query-only and constant terms cancel in the softmax and only a
per-channel bias on the keys remains. Injecting a separate embedding before
every group attention layer is a possible alternative; we do not adopt it
without evidence that the input embedding is diluted across layers. As a
check, linear probes on the final encoder states must recover the index of
each series and of its source series; the alternative is considered only if
they fail. We do not reuse RoPE here. RoPE encodes relative offsets, which suit lags but not
series identities, whereas the target requires the absolute index of the
series itself and of each source series. Channel order is random in the
synthetic training data, so the index carries no information about the
dynamics; on real data it is the order in which the channels are supplied.
Because forecasts of the head-bearing arms may depend on that order, we report
their real-data forecasts under several permutations of the channels.

\paragraph{Which arms use the channel index.}
Arms without an equation head, namely the preliminary KernelSynth candidates
and our generator without a head in Studies~A and~B, use the unmodified
Chronos-2, exactly as the completed LMC runs. The comparison between LMC and
our generator therefore differs only in the generator, and the completed LMC
runs serve as the LMC arms of both studies. Arms with an equation head use the
channel-index embedding, which the head requires
(\path{configs/experiments/prior_baseline_frozen_head.json}; the runner refuses
an equation head without it). The embedding is part of the equation-head
intervention: the contrast between our generator with and without the head
measures the head and the channel index together.

\subsubsection{Study design}

\paragraph{Reference and shared backbone.}
The LMC-Synth reference is the official TimePFN generator
\citep{taga2025timepfn}, pinned to the revision recorded in the executed
notebook. We use its
independent KernelSynth stage before the correlated LMC stage. Both generator
arms train the same randomly initialized Chronos-2 backbone
\citep{ansari2025chronos2}, with the channel-index embedding added only in
equation-head arms (Section~\ref{sec:chronos-explainability-modifications}),
with six synthetic channels, 96 context steps,
96 forecast steps, and an effective batch of 16 multivariate examples.
Here an \emph{example}, or \emph{window}, means 192 consecutive generated time
steps of one six-channel sample, with all channels aligned: the first
$96\times6$ values are the context given to Chronos-2 and the following
$96\times6$ are the forecast targets. A batch contains 16 such windows,
represented by context and target tensors of shape $16\times96\times6$ each.
One optimizer update consumes one batch. A source trajectory is a longer
generated series from which a window is cut. In the independent KernelSynth
stage, a six-channel sample combines six univariate source trajectories; in
the correlated LMC stage it comes from one six-channel trajectory. The LMC
bank can supply multiple windows from its source trajectories, whereas the
current proposed-generator configuration contributes one window from each
newly generated six-channel trajectory. Thus equal window counts do not imply
equal numbers of distinct trajectories.

We chose six channels for \emph{both} synthetic generator arms. This retains
cross-series dependencies while allowing the six trajectories, their
dependencies, and their forecast errors to be inspected together in an
individual example. It also reduces generation and training cost on the
available RTX~4070. At 160 channels, quantitative metrics remain possible,
but an individual multivariate case is harder to inspect. We consider six a
tractable first test of the proposed mechanisms, not evidence that findings
will transfer to 160 channels. The published TimePFN uses 160 channels and
another, much smaller architecture; our comparison is an explicit adaptation,
not a reproduction of its reported result. The LMC's correlated stage and
our generator both supply six series per window, with cross-series relations
possible. Unpublished generator parameters are labelled as local
choices. The recorded source precedence is: this study's explicit
Chronos-2/six-channel decisions; subsequent author guidance dated
6 November 2025; the official code revision of 29 July 2025; then the
original article. Where a numerical value is absent from those sources,
it is our choice. The 2019 outcomes already inspected during development
cannot be treated as an untouched test after selecting a generator with them.
The proposed generator's parameter ranges and any tuning trials
must be recorded with their development-only selection data and compute
cost. The LMC reference retains the published implementation and the authors'
subsequent training guidance, with our six-channel Chronos-2 adaptation,
without selecting its hyperparameters from these evaluation outcomes. For the
proposed generator, the one declared development choice is between 25\% and
50\% KernelSynth channels; the remaining generator settings are fixed. The
reference generator comparison uses forecasting loss only. Two further
interventions on our generator are evaluated below: an auxiliary equation
head and an additional within-stage difficulty curriculum. We denote the
second arm the \emph{AST multiseries generator} (called \texttt{own} in the
runner). Its treatment follows the sampled dependency topology: channels
with an external source use Shao--Wu and at least one external lag, while
self-only channels draw Jacobian or Heaps with equal probability. KernelSynth
channels count as possible external sources. Under random topology with up to
two external sources, the expected shares before hybrid-family effects are
approximately two thirds Shao--Wu and one sixth each Jacobian and Heaps.
Tree-based AST treatments use trees with one to six operators, maximum lag 16,
maximum degree four, and
parameter magnitudes between 0.05 and 1, matching the canonicalizer's AST complexity
range. Each accepted trajectory generates exactly 192 recurrence steps,
providing the 96-step context and 96-step target after discarding the
initialization history. Initial histories are sampled independently for each
channel from the declared random mixture of constant, white-noise, and cosine
components. These settings define the AST component in the preliminary
mixture study below; the completed LMC runs retain their original recorded
configurations.

\paragraph{Preliminary KernelSynth-mixture selection.}
Before Studies~A and~B, we select the channel composition of the proposed
generator in a separate development study. For each of the six channels in
each synthetic example, the hybrid generator independently chooses a
univariate KernelSynth trajectory with probability
$p\in\{0.25,0.50\}$; otherwise it generates that channel from an AST
equation. An AST channel may depend on previous values of a KernelSynth
channel or another AST channel. KernelSynth channels contribute to the
forecasting loss but have no known AST skeleton or innovation-scale target
from our equation generator; their auxiliary equation losses are masked in
later head-bearing runs. The configured probability is recorded with the run,
and the realized channel families are counted in the corpus manifest. The two
candidates differ only in this channel-family probability.
This channel-wise mixture differs from the LMC reference's KernelSynth-first
then correlated-LMC training schedule.

We train two randomly initialized Chronos-2 models, one per value of $p$,
without an auxiliary equation head or additional within-stage curriculum.
Both candidates draw every example with six active channels and the same
AST complexity range, without difficulty strata or ordering. Thus the
preliminary comparison changes only the KernelSynth probability.
Each uses the same backbone, seed policy, 16-example six-channel 96/96
windows, two-phase optimizer schedule, final
checkpoint rule, and the Study~A budget of 445,433 updates split into
346,996 first-phase and 98,437 second-phase updates. AST channels may have
cross-series dependencies from the first update. Synthetic corpora are
prepared offline and their generation costs reported separately. Apart from
the KernelSynth choice, the AST mechanism distribution, initialization law,
window rule, and other generator parameters are held fixed. Both candidates
are evaluated on identical real-data development origins. A short feasibility
pilot may assess generation failures,
memory, and throughput, but cannot choose $p$ from forecasting outcomes or
change the two declared candidate values.

Ibirapuera and Bahia are development datasets for choosing $p$. To avoid
averaging errors in different physical units, first compute each channel's
MAE relative to its 24-hour seasonal-naive MAE, then take the geometric mean
within each dataset. The prespecified selection score is the geometric mean
of the two dataset-level relative errors, giving the datasets equal weight.
The lowest score selects $p$; an exact tie selects the smaller $p$. We report
both dataset-level summaries and every channel, including disagreements,
alongside the realized mixture, data-generation cost, and synthetic validation
curve. The existing 2019 results have already been inspected; if their origins
are used for this selection, they are explicitly development evidence and
cannot support an independent final claim. Separate eligible evaluation
periods or regions for both datasets, with frozen origins and missing-data
rules, must be identified before inspecting the selected models' outcomes.
Until that confirmation is available, subsequent real-data comparisons are
reported as exploratory. The selected $p$ and complete selection record are
frozen before preparing the definitive AST/hybrid arms of Studies~A and~B.
The unselected candidate remains a development result, not an extra
confirmatory comparison with LMC.

\paragraph{Study A: controlled corpus.}
We prepare one offline sequence of batches per generator with equal numbers of
multivariate examples, channel count, context, and horizon. Generation time is
measured separately. LMC windows come from its fixed bank and window rule;
our generator contributes fresh trajectories with one window per trajectory.
The study controls exposed examples, not unique trajectories or the window
rule. Three Chronos-2 runs are planned: LMC without an equation head, our
generator without a head, and our generator with a head. The equation-head
run uses the same ordered forecasting windows as its no-head control; the
offline corpus additionally stores equation and innovation-scale targets for
the AST channels, a six-channel applicability mask, teacher provenance, and
hashes. KernelSynth channels remain in the forecasting loss and have their
auxiliary losses masked.
The three runs share
the initialization policy, effective batch, optimizer, update count, and
step-indexed learning-rate schedule. This controls exposure and deliberately
does not measure the value of producing more examples per hour. The
prespecified exposure budget is 445,433 updates, divided into 346,996
first-phase and 98,437 second-phase updates with 16 examples per update. For
LMC these phases are independent and correlated, respectively; the runner
uses the same two phase names for our arm, although both of its phases can
contain cross-series dependencies. These
counts follow the finite-window rules of the pinned TimePFN implementation:
its independent and correlated banks supply 5,551,945 and 1,575,000 candidate
windows, respectively. The final incomplete batches discard nine and eight
windows, leaving 7,126,928 exposed examples. Thus the controlled LMC run
traverses each stage's available full batches once; our generator supplies
the same number of examples from fresh trajectories. The configuration,
seed list, and checkpoint rule are recorded before the definitive runs.
The LMC reference changes its data distribution from independent KernelSynth
to correlated LMC at the phase boundary. Our generator permits cross-series
dependencies in both phases, including its first batch. It keeps the same
update allocation and two-cycle learning-rate schedule for this comparison;
those optimizer boundaries do not imply an independent-data phase for our
generator. Study~A matches exposure and optimization schedules, but the
generator families and their temporal ordering differ. Thus any forecast
difference cannot be attributed to the mechanism family alone. The additional
curriculum intervention below is distinct from LMC's stage progression.

\paragraph{Study B: complete pipeline.}
The second study fixes one physical machine, including its RTX~4070, CPU,
RAM, and storage, and a ten-hour active wall-clock budget before any arm is
trained. Five runs execute sequentially on that machine: LMC without a head;
our generator without either additional intervention; our generator with the
head only; our generator with the additional curriculum only; and our
generator with both. Each receives the full, identical active-time budget.
All begin with random Chronos-2 backbone weights
and an empty experiment-specific data cache. The clock includes preparation
and generation, worker startup, batch waiting, optimization, in-training
validation, and checkpointing. Concurrent generation and training count once
by elapsed time. Installation and final real-data evaluation are measured
separately. LMC can reuse windows from a trajectory generated during its
run; the proposed generator can introduce fresh trajectories. We record
generated raw trajectories or an explicitly labelled bound, windows exposed,
updates, cache occupancy, repeated windows, batch wait, throughput, sampled
GPU utilization, and peak memory rather than requiring those quantities to
match. Cold-cache
pilots test feasibility; their caches are excluded from definitive runs.
We distinguish a fully precomputed offline corpus, a finite bank filled
on demand and cached, and continuous generation of new trajectories.
Short pilots of these regimes measure throughput, exposed batch wait,
memory, and failure rate. The regime for each arm is chosen from these
operational measures before examining downstream test errors; the pilot
selection rule and its cost must be documented. The actual policy for
trajectory reuse is recorded rather than inferred from the word ``online.''

The time-limited learning-rate schedule must finish within this budget.
Its phase boundaries, rate values, and division between independent and
LMC stages are frozen beforehand. Indexing scheduler progress by active
elapsed time has published precedent \citep{kaddour2023notrainnogain}, but
its application to these pipelines is our adaptation. The fixed split
of 0.7790082908091677 is the fraction of updates in the historical
LMC run; reusing it to divide wall time and our generator's learning-rate
phases is a local choice, not an author recommendation. All five runs use the
same optimizer-time split, although only LMC switches from independent to
correlated data at that boundary. The LMC arm can revisit its
finite window bank. Reaching a safety update limit before the deadline
invalidates the run instead of ending it as if the budget were spent.
The primary checkpoint is the last update started before the deadline;
an in-flight optimizer step is allowed to finish and its overrun is reported.
Intermediate checkpoints
serve resumption and diagnosis and are not selected using real test errors.
Synthetic validation may diagnose training or select a separately labelled
checkpoint, but the primary checkpoint remains the final-budget checkpoint;
real datasets never select training checkpoints.
The first-phase terminal checkpoint is also retained to diagnose training
progress; for LMC specifically it helps assess the subsequent LMC stage. It
is not the primary result. A
controlled interruption saves weights, optimizer, scheduler, random state,
stage, and accumulated active elapsed time. Time spent shut down does not
consume the budget; startup and data work after resumption do. A run that
cannot restore those states reproducibly is restarted under a new run ID.
Ten hours is a local cost choice informed by the order of magnitude of the
first LMC experiment, not a duration prescribed by TimePFN. Its final
fixed-update checkpoint was actually recorded at about 13.23 hours of
elapsed training time; therefore ten hours does not reproduce that full run
or guarantee traversal of its entire finite bank. The historical LMC run
remains an exploratory reference, not a matched-time result. The exact
configuration hash and seed list must be recorded before definitive runs.

\paragraph{Auxiliary equation head.}
For each AST-generated series, a frozen six-series,
\texttt{transformer\_skeleton} canonicalization encoder supplies one embedding
of that series's executed equation. The target describes the skeleton,
including cross-series references by absolute channel index and lags, without
requiring parameter prediction; the channel-index embedding of
Section~\ref{sec:chronos-explainability-modifications} makes the absolute index
learnable. The teacher is trained on equations drawn by the forecasting
generator itself, one channel per sampled system, encoded as executed: Shao--Wu
equations with their tanh factors and gain scale, Jacobian equations with their
scale, and Heaps autoregressions
(\path{configs/canonicalization_multiseries_six_series.json}). Its input
capacity, \texttt{max\_nodes}${}=65$, covers the largest equation of each
treatment (22 nodes for Shao--Wu, 15 for Jacobian, and 65 for a 16-lag Heaps
autoregression), and its decoding length covers the longest canonical target.
Its repository, revision, checkpoint digest, vocabulary, and embedding
normalization are recorded with each run; the head compares L2-normalized
embeddings. The teacher's weights do not receive gradients. An equation that
still exceeded the teacher's capacity would have its skeleton target masked
rather than truncated, and the corpus manifest counts covered and masked
targets separately for each treatment. The head reads the backbone through a
learned attention pooling over the context tokens of its series, initialized
as their mean. A second auxiliary output predicts the innovation standard
deviation for each series. Since the forecasting backbone normalizes each
input series, the loss uses the standard deviation divided by the per-series
standardization scale, with a declared positive floor, on a log scale. The
pinned Chronos-2 instance normalization uses the population standard deviation
of the observed context, substituting $10^{-5}$ for an exactly zero scale, and
then applies arcsinh; the ratio remains identifiable because arcsinh is a
fixed invertible map. The head also reports the prediction in original units,
multiplied back by the context scale, and validation records its relative
error $|\hat\sigma/\sigma-1|$, which is the same in either unit. Both auxiliary losses and their weights
are declared before the definitive head-bearing runs, using only synthetic
development data to check numerical stability and target scale. They are not
chosen using real test errors, and the LMC reference has no corresponding
auxiliary head or loss. At
real-data inference the auxiliary heads and teacher are unused; all variants
forecast through the same Chronos-2 backbone and forecast head. We report
embedding alignment and innovation-scale prediction on disjoint synthetic
data as diagnostics, separately from real-data forecasting quality.

\paragraph{Additional curriculum and paired controls.}
All runs have two optimizer phases, but only LMC changes from independent to
correlated data at their boundary. Here ``additional curriculum'' means
ordering our generator's examples by difficulty \emph{within} each optimizer
phase, while allowing cross-series dependencies throughout. The planned
difficulty factor is expression complexity. Every example has six active
channels throughout all three strata. The strata allow at most two, four,
and six AST operators, respectively.
Within each optimizer phase, the curriculum and its control use the same predetermined
counts of examples from each difficulty stratum and, where feasible, the same
indexed examples. The control interleaves the strata throughout the phase;
the curriculum places them in a frozen easy-to-hard order. This compares
ordering rather than changing the overall difficulty mixture. The difficulty
levels, quotas, ordering, and randomization are fixed before training.
For a time-limited run, different generation costs can still change how many
examples from each stratum are actually consumed before the deadline. We
record those realized counts and treat the throughput difference as part of
the complete-pipeline result; it receives no compensating time. A separate
exposure-controlled curriculum comparison would be needed to isolate ordering
from this throughput effect.

\paragraph{Planned contrasts and uncertainty.}
The preliminary mixture study adds two development runs per initialization
seed to the eight main-study runs: three in Study~A and five in Study~B. The
two completed LMC runs, which use the same unmodified backbone as our arms
without a head, are the LMC arms of both studies.
The selected mixture is fixed for all proposed-generator arms in the main
studies. The
primary complete-pipeline decision remains LMC versus our generator without
either additional intervention. For the equation-head question, the
prespecified principal contrast is our generator with versus without the head,
both without the additional curriculum, under the equal-time budget. The
corresponding controlled-corpus contrast asks whether the result persists at
equal exposure. The curriculum contrast without a head, its contrast with a
head, and their difference-in-differences interaction are secondary and
reported without a separate confirmatory winner claim. All comparisons use
the same real evaluation origins and channel-wise errors. Paired temporal-block
intervals quantify uncertainty over those origins, not training-seed
variability. The seed count has not yet been frozen; if only one seed is
affordable, all intervention effects and the interaction are exploratory, and
small differences cannot be distinguished from training randomness. Any
confirmatory seed-replicated analysis requires a prespecified seed list and
additional full runs, reported separately from the eight-run-per-seed plan.

\paragraph{Real tasks and information access.}
Ibirapuera, S\~ao Paulo, provides hourly O$_3$, NO, NO$_2$, CO, and
PM$_{2.5}$ observations associated with \citet{seguel2022photochemical}.
The Bahia subset of \citet{deng2023harmonized} provides disaggregated
electricity demand, modeled wind and solar capacity factors, and hydropower
inflow. The modeled potentials are not observed generation. Neither source
paper reports our exact forecasting task. The exploratory protocol evaluates
every eligible 2019 origin on a 24-hour grid, with 96 context and 96 forecast
hours. Each channel needs at least 80\% observed values in both halves;
missing future targets are excluded. Synthetic-pretrained Chronos-2 models
receive real channels jointly without real-data fitting (zero-shot).
DLinear \citep{zeng2023transformers} is fitted locally to earlier real years
and validated on 2018; it is a supervised domain reference. Persistence and
repetition of the preceding 24 hours are untrained controls. If a past
same-hour value is missing, the seasonal control searches earlier 24-hour
lags and finally uses the last observed context value. All methods
must use identical origins and masks. The seasonal control has now been
evaluated for the 2019 exploratory windows and is reported in the tables. Its
score is an external adequacy check: we report whether each synthetic arm
beats it in each channel and on each dataset's scale-free summary. Failure
of either arm is
reported irrespective of which generator wins the head-to-head comparison.
The current 2019 results are exploratory because they were inspected during
development. A confirmatory period or region will be selected before
examining its model outcomes; another state in the Deng dataset is a
candidate, but its identity, years, channels, and eligibility rule are not
yet frozen. We will not label the existing 2019 comparison confirmatory.

\paragraph{Metrics and decision rule.}
For channel $c$, let $\mathcal O_c$ contain its observed target cells:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{MAE}_c=\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal O_c}|\hat y_i-y_i|}
{|\mathcal O_c|},\qquad
\operatorname{WAPE}_c=100\,
\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal O_c}|\hat y_i-y_i|}
{\sum_{i\in\mathcal O_c}|y_i|}.
\label{eq:foundation-prior-errors}
\end{equation}
WAPE is undefined for a zero denominator and can exceed 100\%. It avoids
pointwise division by zero for wind and solar. Raw MAE is never averaged
across unlike physical units.

For each dataset separately, the scale-free summary is the geometric mean
of its channels' MAE divided by their respective 24-hour seasonal-control
MAE. We report the Ibirapuera and Bahia summaries in separate rows and do
not pool them into one score. Within each dataset, the proposed-generator
summary divided by the LMC summary below one favors our generator. A paired
temporal-block interval wholly below or above one supports that dataset's
direction; an interval containing one is inconclusive. A joint claim that
one pipeline is better on both chosen datasets requires the same supported
direction in both. Opposite directions are reported as mixed, and any
inconclusive dataset prevents a joint winner claim. The equation-head
principal contrast uses the same per-dataset summary, with the no-head
version of our generator as denominator. Curriculum and interaction
contrasts remain secondary; their results are also reported by dataset.
The block rule, zero-denominator policy, and evaluation periods must be
frozen before a confirmatory decision. With a single model seed, if that is
the eventual budget, these intervals describe variation across temporal
origins, not across initializations or new domains. The controlled-corpus
study uses the same per-dataset descriptive tables, but is secondary to
the complete-pipeline comparison.
