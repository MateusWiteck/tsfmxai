\subsection{Nonlinear generation using Shao--Wu moment contraction}
\label{sec:implemented-shao-wu}

This family combines a published sufficient condition with a project-specific
AST construction. The mathematical result supplies the target property; the
construction supplies computable bounds, a scaling rule, and an initialization
policy that satisfy it.

\subsubsection{Mathematical result used: Theorem 5.1}
\label{sec:implemented-shao-wu-theory}

\citet[Section~5, Theorem~5.1, electronic reprint pp.~11--12]{shao2007spectral}
consider a measurable, time-homogeneous recurrence with i.i.d. innovations.
For our second-moment specialization, let $\mathbf{s}_t$ stack the $L$ most
recent vectors of $N$ series and write
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf x_{t+1}=\mathcal R_{\mathcal T}(\mathbf{s}_t;
                                      \boldsymbol\varepsilon_{t+1}).
 \label{eq:implemented-gmc-recurrence}
\end{equation}
The hypotheses are a finite second moment at one fixed reference history
and, for every pair of histories, the global inequality
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 \|\mathcal R_{\mathcal T}(\mathbf{s};\boldsymbol\varepsilon)
      -\mathcal R_{\mathcal T}(\mathbf{s}';\boldsymbol\varepsilon)\|_{L^2}
 &\leq\sum_{d=0}^{L-1}a_d^{\mathrm{GMC}}
                   \|\mathbf{s}_d-\mathbf{s}'_d\|_2,\\
 a_d^{\mathrm{GMC}}&\geq0,\qquad
 c_{\mathrm{GMC}}:=\sum_{d=0}^{L-1}a_d^{\mathrm{GMC}}<1.
\end{split}
\label{eq:implemented-gmc-condition}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathbf{s}_d\in\mathbb R^N$ is one lag block. The theorem yields a
causal strictly stationary solution and geometric moment contraction. In the
paper's coupling, two stationary versions have independent innovation
histories through the origin and share all subsequent innovations. Their
expected squared difference satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E\|\mathbf x_{\tau+v}-\mathbf x'_{\tau+v}\|_2^2
 \leq C_{\mathrm{GMC}}\rho_{\mathrm{GMC}}^{v},
 \qquad C_{\mathrm{GMC}}>0,\quad 0<\rho_{\mathrm{GMC}}<1.
 \label{eq:implemented-gmc-forgetting}
\end{equation}
This is the GMC definition in Section~3, Eq.~(3.1), p.~5. It expresses fading
influence of the replaced past. Recent lags remain active inputs, and fresh
innovations sustain random variation. The full theorem allows other moment
orders; the implementation uses order two.

\subsubsection{Our construction: making the hypotheses computable}
\label{sec:implemented-shao-wu-construction}

All operations below are implemented in
\path{src/tsfmxai/generators/shao_wu.py}. They define our synthetic prior and
certificate, using Theorem~5.1 as their justification.

\paragraph{1. Build a bounded, time-homogeneous drift.}
For each target, sample a base tree with parameter and lag leaves, addition,
and multiplication. Replace each lag leaf $x_{j,t-\ell}$ with
$\tanh(x_{j,t-\ell})$. The resulting drift has finite global amplitude and
derivative bounds because $|\tanh z|\leq1$ and $|\tanh'(z)|\leq1$.
This transformation bounds individual feedback factors before products are
formed. The sampler retains a finite expression for every target and excludes
explicit time leaves. The final recurrence adds independent Gaussian
innovations with covariance $\sigma_\varepsilon^2 I$.

\paragraph{2. Propagate bounds through the AST.}
For each subexpression $e$, the recursive calculation returns an amplitude
bound $A(e)$ and a matrix $D(e)$ of nonnegative absolute derivative bounds,
indexed by source series and lag offset. Table~\ref{tab:ast-global-bounds}
gives the implemented rules. These bounds hold over the full real lag-state
space, allowing verification before drawing a trajectory.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Subexpression & Amplitude bound $A$ & Derivative bounds $D$ \\
\midrule
Constant $c$ & $|c|$ & $0$ \\
Lag leaf & $+\infty$ & $1$ at its source/lag, $0$ elsewhere \\
$\tanh(e)$ & $1$ & $D(e)$ \\
$e_1+e_2$ & $A(e_1)+A(e_2)$ & $D(e_1)+D(e_2)$ \\
$e_1e_2$ & $A(e_1)A(e_2)$ & $A(e_2)D(e_1)+A(e_1)D(e_2)$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Conservative global bound propagation in our Shao--Wu constructor.
The automatic sampler wraps every lag leaf in $\tanh$ before products are
bounded. A zero derivative contributes zero even if the other amplitude
bound is infinite.}
\label{tab:ast-global-bounds}
\end{table}

\paragraph{3. Combine channels and allocate the gain budget.}
For lag offset $d$, stack the bounds from all targets into a nonnegative
$N\times N$ matrix $\mathbf D_d$, whose entry $(k,j)$ bounds the effect of
source $j$ on target $k$. Define
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde a_d=\|\mathbf D_d\|_2,\qquad
 \widetilde c=\sum_{d=0}^{L-1}\widetilde a_d,
 \qquad
 \eta_{\mathrm{GMC}}=
 \eta_{\mathrm{safe}}\min\{1,g_\star/\widetilde c\}.
 \label{eq:implemented-gmc-scaling}
\end{equation}
The matrix norm is the largest singular value. If $\widetilde c=0$, the
minimum in Eq.~\eqref{eq:implemented-gmc-scaling} is defined as 1. The
defaults are $g_\star=0.8$ and $\eta_{\mathrm{safe}}=0.95$.
Multiply every output tree by $\eta_{\mathrm{GMC}}$, giving
$a_d^{\mathrm{GMC}}=\eta_{\mathrm{GMC}}\widetilde a_d$ and
$c_{\mathrm{GMC}}\leq0.76<1$.

This block calculation accounts for interactions between channels. Its
justification is the entrywise bound on the absolute Jacobian block, which
bounds its Euclidean operator norm by $\|\mathbf D_d\|_2$. Integrating these
derivatives between any two histories and applying the triangle inequality
gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:implemented-gmc-condition}. Additive innovations cancel
in this comparison because the same innovation is used on both sides.

\paragraph{4. Verify the moment condition and control initialization.}
Let $B_{\mathrm{GMC}}$ be the Euclidean norm of the final componentwise
amplitude bounds. The stationary solution satisfies
$\|\mathbf x_t\|_{L^2}\leq B_{\mathrm{GMC}}+\sigma_\varepsilon\sqrt N$.
In particular, the required anchor moment is finite at the zero history.
Starting each lag block in $[-r_0,r_0]^N$ gives the coupling bound
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
 M_0=r_0\sqrt N+B_{\mathrm{GMC}}+\sigma_\varepsilon\sqrt N,\\
 \max_{0\leq d<L}
 \|\mathbf x_{b_{\mathrm{burn}}-d}-\mathbf x^{\mathrm{stat}}_{b_{\mathrm{burn}}-d}\|_{L^2}
 \leq M_0c_{\mathrm{GMC}}^{K}.
\end{gathered}
 \label{eq:implemented-gmc-burnin}
\end{equation}
Here $b_{\mathrm{burn}}=L-1+KL$ counts burn-in steps from an initial history ending at zero.
The maximum lagwise $L^2$ error contracts by at most $c_{\mathrm{GMC}}$
after each complete block of $L$ steps: once a block is generated, every lag
entering the next block already has the reduced error bound. The extra $L-1$
steps ensure that the oldest observation in the retained history has also
completed $K$ contraction blocks. The bound concerns each lag block, with
the maximum outside the expectation; a bound on the entire stacked
Euclidean state gains a factor $\sqrt L$.

For $0<c_{\mathrm{GMC}}<1$ and $M_0>\delta_{\mathrm{init}}$, choose
$K=\lceil\log(\delta_{\mathrm{init}}/M_0)/\log(c_{\mathrm{GMC}})\rceil$.
The code uses $K=0$ when the initial bound already meets the tolerance, and
$K=1$ when the gain is zero and a contraction block is needed. The default
per-lag tolerance is $\delta_{\mathrm{init}}=10^{-4}$. Initialization simulates
the Gaussian-driven recurrence for $b_{\mathrm{burn}}$ steps and retains the final $L$
observations. Fresh, independently seeded innovations generate the returned
trajectory. This is approximate stationary initialization with an explicit
residual bound; the stationary law itself is supplied by the theorem.

\paragraph{A worked construction.}
Consider our example drift $0.3\tanh(x_t)\tanh(x_{t-1})$.
Its amplitude bound is 0.3, and both lag derivative bounds are 0.3, giving
$\widetilde c=0.6$. The default safety factor yields the executed recurrence
\begin{equation}
 x_{t+1}=0.285\tanh(x_t)\tanh(x_{t-1})+\varepsilon_{t+1},
 \qquad \varepsilon_{t+1}\sim\mathcal N(0,0.05^2).
 \label{eq:implemented-gmc-example}
\end{equation}
Now $c_{\mathrm{GMC}}=0.57$, $M_0=1.335$ for $r_0=1$, and $K=17$ gives
$b_{\mathrm{burn}}=35$ burn-in steps. The resulting record retains the scaled AST, each
lag gain, the amplitude bound, the innovation scale, the burn-in length,
and the residual error bound. These quantities are tested analytically and
through reproducibility and numerical derivative checks.

\paragraph{Scope of this construction.}
The implemented constructor requires both finite global drift amplitudes
and finite global derivative bounds. This selects a convenient bounded-drift
subfamily of the theorem: unbounded polynomial feedback is rejected, while
unbounded linear drift can be handled by the separate VAR family. Conservative
bounds and scaling can weaken useful signal relative to the innovations.
Accordingly, low-amplitude diagnostics remain relevant even when the GMC
certificate holds. Gaussian tails permit finite amplitude-threshold crossings;
the certificate controls dependence and moments rather than imposing a hard
trajectory range.

The inequalities above describe the construction in exact arithmetic. The
implementation evaluates its bounds and singular values in floating point,
retains a strict gain margin, and checks the resulting certificate and sampled
derivatives. These checks provide numerical validation of the implementation;
their precision is governed by the numerical routines used.
