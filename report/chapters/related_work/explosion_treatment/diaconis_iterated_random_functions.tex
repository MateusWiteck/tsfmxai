\subsubsection{Stationary Laws of Random Function Iterations}

\citet{diaconis1999iterated} represent a Markov chain as repeated applications
of random Lipschitz maps,
$\mathbf{s}_{t+1}=\mathcal F_{\xi_{t+1}}(\mathbf{s}_t)$. On a complete
separable metric space, integrability conditions together with contraction on
average,

\begin{equation}
    \mathbb E[\log K_{\xi}]<0,
\end{equation}

where $K_{\xi}$ is the Lipschitz factor of the sampled map, yield a unique
stationary distribution and geometric convergence to it. The benchmark's
uniform certificate $K_{\xi}\le\gamma<1$ for every allowed innovation is a
stronger condition than contraction on average. This result explains why a
contractive recurrence with persistent random innovations can keep fluctuating
while its distribution becomes independent of the initial history.
