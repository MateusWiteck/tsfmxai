## Dataset Details
- **Dataset names:** See paper/code
- **Synthetic or real:** Includes synthetic and real forecasting settings
- **Input shape:** History/context
- **Output shape:** Forecast horizon
- **Generation mechanism:** The synthetic dataset is an hourly load-forecasting generator. Each example has two weeks of hourly target values and covariates. The model receives the first week of target/load values plus both weeks of covariates, and predicts the second week of target/load values.
- **Synthetic dataset size:** 120,000 examples: 100,000 train, 10,000 validation, and 10,000 test.
- **Synthetic covariates:** Hour of day, day of week, month, holiday, temperature, and two uncorrelated noise features. Covariates are available for both the history and forecast horizon, representing a perfect covariate forecast.
- **Load generation details:** The generator samples month, start weekday, start hour, and a base load. Month adds a sinusoidal seasonal effect. A daily curve is generated from sinusoidal components plus a random 24-hour pattern. Workday, Saturday, and Sunday patterns differ through multiplicative and additive changes. Holidays force the Sunday pattern. Temperature is generated by a random walk and multiplicatively rescales load. Final target noise is Gaussian with mean 0 and standard deviation 0.05.
- **Forecast framing:** `168` hourly history steps -> `168` hourly future steps.

## Ground-Truth Explanation
- **Known mask?** Yes, but not as a simple binary mask. The paper creates ground-truth SHAP values for the synthetic generator.
- **Mask/attribution shape:** Feature-group SHAP values over a one-week forecast horizon. Inputs include past load plus covariate time series; load is grouped by input day and covariates are treated as feature groups.
- **Mask generation:** The authors run the SHAP Permutation Explainer on the actual data-generation process rather than on a learned model. Missing/absent inputs are resampled 1,000 times using the generator, then alternative target curves are averaged to estimate expected targets for feature subsets.
- **Dependency handling:** When resampling absent inputs, the paper prevents unrealistic combinations. For example, if day of week is active, hour of day is not resampled; if past load is active, calendar information, the multiplier, and day patterns that determine the past load are not resampled.
- **Important caveat:** The ground truth is “true to the data” under SHAP assumptions, not necessarily a causal binary mask. It is best for evaluating SHAP-value agreement and feature-dependence plots, not for simple Precision@K over binary lag masks.
- **Synthetic-generation references cited:** The ground-truth explanation construction relies on Shapley/SHAP references, SHAP Permutation Explainer, “true to the model or true to the data” discussion, TimeSHAP, WindowSHAP, and Owen values for grouped/coalitional feature structure. The synthetic load generator itself appears to be introduced by this paper and is released with ground-truth explanations at `https://github.com/KIT-IAI/SHAPformer`.

## Explanation Method
- **Type:** Intrinsic/post-hoc hybrid
- **Family:** SHAP-style attribution without sampling
- **Target:** Time-series inputs / lags / features
- **Attribution scope:** Covariate Attribution and coarse Temporal Attribution; partial Time-Covariate Attribution through grouped time-series features. The method explains feature groups such as past-load days and exogenous covariates, so it is stronger for feature/group attribution than for pointwise timestamp masks.

## Evaluation
- **Forecasting metrics:** RMSE on the synthetic dataset table; the broader experiments also report MAE, MSE, RMSE, and MAPE.
- **Explanation metrics:** Synthetic explanations are evaluated against ground-truth SHAP values computed from the known data-generating process. The paper compares SHAPformer with Transformer + SHAP Permutation Explainer, Transformer + custom masker, TFT, persistence, linear regression, and XGBoost-style baselines depending on experiment.
- **Runtime metrics:** Training time and inference time are reported, because the main claim is sampling-free SHAP-style attribution for Transformer forecasting.
- **Evaluation summary check:** The earlier summary was right that this is forecasting + explanation + synthetic ground truth, but it understated the SHAP-specific nature of the benchmark. The ground truth is not a binary causal mask; it is a SHAP-value target generated from the known synthetic mechanism, so it is useful for attribution-value benchmarking rather than only mask overlap.
- **Evaluation references cited:** Anchors the method and evaluation in Shapley values (Shapley, 1953), SHAP (Lundberg and Lee, 2017), algorithms for estimating Shapley attributions (Chen, Covert, Lundberg, and Lee, 2023), true-to-model vs true-to-data explanation discussion (Chen, Janizek, Lundberg, and Lee, 2020; Frye et al.), TimeSHAP (Bento et al.), WindowSHAP (Nayebi et al., 2023), Molnar's SHAP book, and Owen values for coalitional/grouped feature structure.
